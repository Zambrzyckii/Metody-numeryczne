# Requirements: presentations

> Extracted from `materials/zasady_zaliczenia_projektu_v1.pdf` (`zasady`), `materials/Projekt_1.pdf` and `PROJECT_BRIEF.md` v1.0 section 1. The PDFs win on conflict. This file is the first two sources of the `presentation-reviewer` criteria; the third is `docs/presentation-style.md`.

## 1. Mandatory conditions for every presentation (`zasady` p. 2)

Missing any of these means the presentation fails until corrected:

- PDF built by compiling **LaTeX**; the compilation is defined in a **`Makefile`**.
- Every slide shows its number as **n/N**, e.g. 8/15.
- **Three groups of slides** are present:
  1. an introduction (about 3 slides) describing how the algorithm works,
  2. a report on AI errors, or the declaration (section 4 below),
  3. the AI review of the presentation (section 5 below).
- A ZIP is submitted with the PDF, the `Makefile` and every file needed to compile.

## 2. Format (`zasady` p. 4–5, 6.1)

- Landscape 16:9 (`aspectratio=169`), legible full-screen on 4K. High resolution is not a substitute for adequately large text.
- About **8 minutes** of speaking. The PDF documents everything required; the talk focuses on the main results.
- Charts are imported as vector graphics. No screenshots of charts.
- Fonts are embedded. The PDF is checked in full-screen mode for legibility, cropping, overlaps and symbols.
- **Title slide** (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 5, 4.1): project topic, author (**Bartosz Zambrzycki**), and whether it is the preliminary („Prezentacja wstępna") or final („Prezentacja końcowa") presentation.

## 3. Content and grading

### 3.1 Preliminary presentation, P2 — 2 points (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 9, table 1)

| Graded element (0.4 each) | Required content |
|---|---|
| Introduction: MACD description | about 3 slides: what MACD is, what it is for, how it is computed; equations of EMA, MACD and SIGNAL with symbol meanings; general meaning of the algorithm without every detail (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 5, 4.1) |
| Way of working and key AI configuration | tool choice, general configuration and organization of work; skills/agents used for code, slides or reviews. One topic described well beats a hundred described briefly (`zasady` p. 4, 5.2) |
| Selected prompt, its result and its evaluation | one concrete prompt with its actual result and our assessment |
| AI errors report or declaration | section 4 below |
| Reviewer, review prompt, final verbal and numeric grade | section 5 below |

The preliminary deck may show partial results of the program, always in connection with the AI tools used (`zasady` p. 4).

### 3.2 Final presentation, P3 — 8 points (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 9, table 2)

| Graded element | Points |
|---|---|
| Introduction and AI collaboration, as in table 1 | 2.0 |
| Clear interpretation of charts A1, B1, C1 and C2 | 1.5 |
| Analysis of the selected trade, chart D1 | 0.5 |
| Whole-series simulation: E1, E2, statistics and conclusions of slide F | 2.0 |
| Experimental part: justification and description (0.5), analysis of the price-vector dataset (1.0), interpretation of the local example (0.5) | 2.0 |

An experimental part consisting only of a comparison with buy-and-hold earns at most 0.5 points (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 9).

The **Methods and results** part presents the algorithm's results together with their discussion (`zasady` p. 4, 5.2). Recommended order for each finding:

1. the most important observation,
2. the algorithm feature that explains it,
3. the conclusion and its range of validity.

Each slide states whether a conclusion concerns a single case or is confirmed on the wider dataset. Grading is based on the quality of the analysis and on compliance, not on profit or on the number of AI tools used (`Projekt_1.pdf` p. 8, 6.2).

## 4. AI errors report (`zasady` p. 4, 5.3)

- About **3 slides** describing an example of incorrect AI behaviour, such as a wrongly executed command, an answer with false information, or generated code containing a bug.
- Source: `docs/ai-log.md`, real findings only (brief 5.4).
- If no AI error was found, use instead the verbatim declaration:

  > W trakcie prac nad projektem nie zauważyłem/zauważyłam błędnego działania AI.

## 5. AI review of the presentation (`zasady` p. 5, 6.3)

Every presentation must be reviewed by an AI tool. The review part is recommended to take about **4 slides** and must contain:

- a short description of how the reviewer was defined and its most important criteria,
- the prompt used to request the review, or its key fragments,
- the final grade generated by the AI, **verbal and numeric on a 1–10 scale**. One slide with both grades is preferred.

Further rules:

- The review may cover the LaTeX sources as well as the PDF's appearance.
- The AI grade does not determine the points awarded.
- The reviewer's quality criteria are built on three sources:
  1. `zasady`,
  2. `Projekt_1.pdf`,
  3. presentation-quality guidelines (`zasady` p. 6, section 7; here: `docs/presentation-style.md`).
